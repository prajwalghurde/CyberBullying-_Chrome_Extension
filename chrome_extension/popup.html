    About Cyberbullying

    Understanding risks and prevention

    What is Cyberbullying?

    Cyberbullying is the use of digital technology (social media, messaging apps, forums, etc.) to harass, threaten, embarrass, or target another person. It includes posting hurtful comments, spreading rumors, sharing private information without consent, or sending threatening messages.

    Common Types of Cyberbullying

    • Harassment: Repeatedly sending offensive, rude, and insulting messages
    • Denigration: Posting untrue or exaggerated statements to damage someone's reputation
    • Impersonation: Pretending to be someone else online to post embarrassing content
    • Outing: Sharing someone's secrets or embarrassing information publicly
    • Exclusion: Intentionally excluding someone from online groups or activities
    • Cyberstalking: Repeatedly sending threatening messages or monitoring someone's online activity

    Risks and Effects

    Cyberbullying can have serious consequences:

    • Emotional Impact: Depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, and feelings of isolation
    • Academic Problems: Decreased school performance and attendance
    • Social Issues: Withdrawal from friends and family, difficulty trusting others
    • Physical Symptoms: Headaches, stomachaches, sleep disturbances
    • Severe Cases: Self-harm thoughts or behaviors in extreme situations

    Prevention Strategies

    For Everyone:

    • Think before you post - consider how your words might affect others
    • Respect others' privacy and obtain consent before sharing their information
    • Stand up against cyberbullying when you witness it
    • Report abusive content to platform administrators

    If You're Being Cyberbullied:

    • Don't respond or retaliate - this often makes it worse
    • Save evidence (screenshots, messages) for reporting
    • Block the bully and adjust privacy settings
    • Talk to someone you trust (friend, family member, teacher)
    • Report the behavior to the platform and/or authorities
    • Remember: It's not your fault, and you don't have to face it alone
